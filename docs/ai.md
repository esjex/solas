# AI policy

I use AI sparingly, and only where I believe it can genuinely provide value and quality. I have the final input on everything I produce, to ensure that your content is clear, accurate, and high quality, and that it meets the highest ethical and legal standards.

For example, I might use AI tools for: 

- **Sourcing general information about the subject matter** (e.g., "Find me an article on how a clutch plate works"). 
- **Verifying general subject matter knowledge** (e.g. "This is my understanding of how a clutch plate works; are there any inaccuracies?")
- **Checking document style and consistency** (e.g. "Make sure I've used _clutch plate_ instead of _clutch disc_ throughout.")

I request and check sources for all information, and I fact-check all technical information with human subject matter experts or human-written resources.

I don't use AI for:

- **Learning or writing about your specific product** (e.g. "My client is making a special type of clutch plate that lights up; why is this useful?" I want to work from _your_ description of your product and the problem it solves.)
- **Making decisions about structure and scope** (e.g. "Which information does the user need first, and why?" This is where human decision-making is essential!)
- **Writing final documentation** (e.g. "Here are some notes, please generate a full page of documentation." Docs are more than a page at a time, and LLM-written docs don't take into consideration the big-picture information architecture of the docs.)

If you have an existing AI policy, I'll work with that. If you don't yet have an AI policy, I'm happy to help you develop one while we work, so we can determine how and when to use LLMs for drafting and editing content, and whether to feed commercially sensitive information about your product or company into AI tools.

I'm not interested in any role that primarily involves babysitting an LLM.

## Ethical disclaimer

I have  ethical concerns about LLMs and generative AI tools, including their [technical inaccuracy](https://openai.com/index/why-language-models-hallucinate/#/), their [data training ethics](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2025/sep/11/google-gemini-ai-training-humans#/), and their [environmental impact](https://www.itpro.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/mistrals-new-sustainability-tracker-tool-shows-the-impact-ai-has-on-the-environment-and-it-makes-for-sober-reading#/). I also have concerns about their impact on human [cognitive functioning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08872#/), [creativity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03703#/), and [mental health](https://www.mentalhealthjournal.org/articles/minds-in-crisis-how-the-ai-revolution-is-impacting-mental-health.html).

My own experiments indicate that AI tools produce technical content that looks great until you pay attention to it. Users tend to pay quite close attention to technical docs, so AI-written docs often need a lot of editing work. I recommend Fabrizio Benedetti's blog post [The writing was always the cheap part](https://passo.uno/real-cost-of-documentation/) for a deep dive into how LLMs can't replace human intutition and expertise.

However, I accept that it's not realistic to completely reject these tools in the current tech landscape. It's a difficult balance. Like everyone else, I'm still figuring it out. 

I'm open to discussing specific approaches with clients who have their own requirements around AI use. I hope my current policy provides reassurance that I use AI tools thoughtfully and intentionally.
