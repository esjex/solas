# APCOA: Building internal developer docs from scratch

**Building a documentation system for an engineering team from the ground up**  
*Freelance technical content designer · Internal documentation*

Turned a large, unsorted collection of notes and transcripts into a searchable docs site the team maintains without me, giving new developers a real onboarding path and freeing the senior engineer from providing constant support.

### The problem

APCOA's engineering team needed support producing and organising internal documentation. Critical knowledge lived either in a few people's heads, or as notes and call transcripts in a shared cloud folder. The consequences were that it took extra time to onboard new developers, and senior engineers had to be a human search index; much of their time went to answering colleagues’ questions and identifying where information was stored.

### The goal

Build a clear, navigable and searchable documentation platform that would let the team find information independently, and free up the senior engineers’ time. This needed to include onboarding material for new starters, and walkthroughs for common tasks to empower the whole team to share project responsibilities.

### Approach

I began by auditing the existing material and interviewing the team to map what they owned, what was documented, and where the knowledge gaps and bottlenecks actually were.

From there:

* **Structured the content.** I categorised everything by product area, then applied the Diátaxis framework, separating explanation, tutorials, how-to guides, and reference. This allowed readers to find information by both topic and the kind of help they needed.  
* **Filled the gaps.** I reviewed each page for completeness and clarity, working with subject-matter experts to surface knowledge that had never been written down.  
* **Chose tooling that fit.** I selected MKDocs, a static site generator aligned to the team's existing tools and workflow, so documentation lives alongside their code and integrates with how they already work, rather than becoming a separate system to maintain.  
* **Set the team up for future work.** I built an AI prompt set anchored to [Good Docs Project templates](https://gitlab.com/tgdp/templates), so that the team could continue to produce quality documentation after the contract ended.  
* **Handed over properly.** I trained engineers, data analysts, and business analysts through one-on-one sessions and written guidance, so the new process would outlast my engagement.

### Outcome

* A functioning static documentation site, organised by product area and depth, with working search.  
* Onboarding documentation covering which products the team owns, each product's internal structure, and how the team works on them.  
* A repeatable AI-assisted workflow for converting notes and transcripts into draft docs.  
* Full team adoption: the team committed to the new process and has continued to use the tools and workflow independently since handover.
